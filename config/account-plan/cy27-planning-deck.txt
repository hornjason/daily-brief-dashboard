CY27 Continuous Account Planning - What is changing and Why
To better align with our customers and partners and drive more meaningful outcomes, we are evolving to Continuous  Account Planning Insights (CAPI)—shifting from a once-a-year exercise to ongoing visibility into account planning progress and quality.
This includes continuous reporting and monitoring of key customer events (e.g., fiscal milestones, product launches, priority shifts), paired with visibility into whether account plans are being updated accordingly. This ensures plans remain current, actionable, and aligned to real-time changes—not just static documents created at a single point in time.
In parallel, planning will look forward on a rolling basis—extending a full year ahead and reviewed quarterly—anchored to the customer’s fiscal cycle rather than our own.  Customers Fiscal year  = budget cycle, this is one of the key reasons for the change.
Over time, this will evolve into self-service dashboards, enabling sales and managers to easily track account planning progress, identify gaps or risks, and take action in real time—without waiting for periodic reports.
By moving to continuous insights, account plans become dynamic tools that guide ongoing engagement, improve accountability, and ultimately drive stronger customer and business outcomes.

OVERVIEW: Account planning phases
Account Planning is a continuous process of activities aimed at progressively understanding the customer’s business drivers, objectives, challenges, and their ecosystem. The goal is to develop a long term strategic plan that strengthens the customer relationship and builds a pipeline of projects that meet their needs.

For the purposes of providing a basic guidance the account planning can be roughly segmented into 4 Account Planning Phases:
Know Your Customer
Know Your Fit
Build Your Strategy
Make It Happen

REMEMBER:

The Account Planning Phases can overlap
How frequently you will be running through these phases depends on the maturity of your business relationship with the customer - if you are unsure what is the correct frequency, work with your sales manager or ask for help
The list of activities within the phases is not exhaustive - you can go above this standard in your account planning activities
The activities within the phases can be performed in any order

GETTING STARTED: Leading account planning sessions
Key for a successful account planning session (or, in fact, for any other meeting) is to have the right people in the room and have the purpose of the session clearly defined. 
Whom should you invite? The Core Account Team should be always present, the Extended Red Hat account team members should be invited based on relevance for the particular customer. As a general rule, if in doubts whether to invite someone or not, invite them - especially brainstorming and discovery sessions in general benefit from diversity of perspectives. (when applicable add your ABM poc and IBM team).
What should be purpose of the account planning session? The objectives of the session depend on where you are with the customer and what is needed, typical account planning sessions topics are:
Discovery session - focused on analyzing the gathered account intelligence & customer initiatives & potential opportunities (see the Business Value Practice team's sample agenda on this slide), typically the objective is uncovering new business
Customer Success & Adoption session - focused on understanding the installed base, maturity of adoption, NPS & customer satisfaction and driving actions to promote consumption
Progress & Update session - focused on reviewing progress in account plan execution and incorporating new information gathered from the customer and partners into the plan
Retrospective session - focused on reviewing the lessons learned from the execution of the account strategy and revisiting the objectives, activities and account intelligence
IBM Joint Account Planning Sessions - any of the above types of sessions, but focused on the particular areas of joint solutions offered with IBM
Global team alignment sessions - specific sessions focused on alignment and level-setting for the global teams working on large international accounts; the objective typically will be sharing information
That is a lot of abbreviations right there ⤴ - if you are not familiar with some of them, check the Red Hat Abbreviations Dictionary

GETTING STARTED:  Creating a plan for new year  (follow-up plan, migrate plan, clone plan)
Despite the account planning being a continuous process, for 2026 and beyond you will be asked to create a follow-up account plan 5 months before your customers fiscal year month start.  The reason is to be able to preserve the history of the account development instead of overwriting it as well as helping us to innovate the structure of the scorecard. Fortunately, this does not mean starting from the scratch!
You trigger the creation of the follow up plan by clicking on the dropdown menu at the right side of the account plan list. Once the menu opens, you select “Create Follow-up”. 
A new window will open where you will need to select the name for the account plan and a timeframe for the plan. You should keep the “Mark as Primary” toggle on. You can add a description of the plan if you wish to.
At the bottom of the window are various checkboxes, which are indicating what sections of the old plan you want to copy into the new plan. Please keep them all checked, unless you want to start some of the sections of the plan from the scratch. Important: the Clone Files may not appear checked - if you want to migrate your files, please check it. 
NO UPGRADE FOR 2026 NEEDED: The plan will at the bottom indicate that there is an “Upgrade Available” - please keep the toggle on - this is the function that will load the new scorecard template. 
Hit Create. You will find the follow up plan in the list of account plans. The old plan will appear linked to the new plan. 
MUST KNOW - READ CAREFULLY:
FILES:
If the option “Clone Files” is not ticked by default, please tick it - otherwise your Files will not appear in the follow up plan (and you will need to re-add them or “Pick from Existing”)
ACTIONS (TASKS):
When you migrate tasks from the original plan to the follow up plan, any OPEN or IN PROGRESS tasks will disappear from the original plan and move to the follow up plan. This action is irreversible!
Any Closed tasks will not migrate and will stay only in the original plan.
Tagged Opportunities:
Closed Won or Closed Lost opportunity tagged in the original plan will not be migrated, otherwise all open and potential opportunities will migrated
Initiatives: 
The timeframe of initiatives will automatically move by 1 year (if they are set for FY). This change is editable.

Understand the Account Plan Structure
Before deep-diving into the account plan in RHSC, it is important to understand that its structure is richer than just one text document. 
The account plan (the functionality is called sometimes ClosePlan or people.ai ClosePlan) has multiple different parts - the core being:
Initiatives
Scorecard
Whitespace Map
Account Maps
Actions
The heart of the plan is the customer-centric view reflected in the Initiatives section. As Initiatives we understand in account planning in RH the customer initiatives, described in the customer's terms and with reflection of the customer's success metrics and target measures. Initiatives section of the plan should have at least 2 customer's initiatives described in customer's terms with success metrics and measures.
The section to capture the account intelligence and the actual account plan is the Scorecard. The Scorecard is the repository of customer intelligence and the plan and its milestones. 
The RH-centric view connecting the account plan with the Technology Decision Points is the Whitespace Map. Whitespace Map is a section of the account plan in the RHSC that focuses on the RH perspective of the business and the internal resources orchestration. It shows the connection between RH's Technical Decision Points and the customer's Business Units (representation of customer’s buying decision structure).
The Actions section is then a helpful tool to drive accountability for execution through tasks assigned to the team members.

GETTING STARTED: Copying a plan
The process to copy a plan combines the creation of a follow up plan and moving of a plan to another account. 
You start by creating the follow-up plan for the plan you want to copy  by selecting this option from the dropdown menu on the very right side of row in the list of account plans.
You change the name of the plan accordingly and adjust any information needed. Please note that the plan will be automatically set with a time frame for next year, but you can change this later on. 
Once the plan is created, refresh your screen.
Now you go to the dropdown menu of your new plan, but this time you select the edit option.
When the pop-up window appears on the right side of your screen, select the dropdown menu next to the account plan's name in the top right corner (little triangle icon).
There is only one option - click on Change account. 
New window opens in which you can type the name of the account and move the plan (more detailed steps in the slide focused  moving of a plan to another account further in this presentation).
This process will result in the new follow up plan being moved to a new account, but the original follow up plan and any antecedent plans will stay in the original account.

In RHSC find the account plan that is on an incorrect account.
Click on the drop down menu on the right side of the account plan that you want to move and select “Edit”.

Select “Change Account”
Another window will open where you can select the correct account by typing its name in the navigation bar.
Tick the box next to the account to which you want to move the account plan.
Click “Submit” - this will move the plan to the selected account.

KNOW YOUR CUSTOMER: Gathering customer intelligence
Gathering the account intelligence is about profoundly understanding your customer leveraging multitude of different resources, contacts and expertise.
Typically, you will need to investigate and understand the following areas of account intelligence:
Industry & Market Landscape: Identify current trends, regulatory changes, and macroeconomic factors impacting the customer’s industry.
Competitive Intelligence: Understand who the customer’s competitors are, what differentiates them, and how your solutions can provide a competitive edge.
Customer's Business Drivers: Look into the customer’s key objectives, pain points, and initiatives driving their strategy and investment decisions.
Customer's Technical Landscape: Assess the current technology stack, adoption levels of your solutions, and any gaps or inefficiencies in their setup.
Stakeholder Mapping: Identify decision-makers, influencers, and champions within the customer’s organization, as well as external partners critical to their success.
When investigating, ensure you:
Use Diverse Data Sources: Leverage internal tools (e.g., CRM, telemetry data) and external research (e.g., market reports, LinkedIn).
Engage with the Customer and Partners: Conduct discovery calls and meetings to validate assumptions and gain deeper insights.
Collaborate Internally: Work with cross-functional teams (e.g., marketing, product, partners) to gather relevant information.
Document Your Findings & Any Open Topics: Gathering account intelligence is a continuous process, and especially exploration of the blank spaces you have identified can render the most interesting results.

KNOW YOUR FIT: Prioritizing customer initiatives
Understanding to the customers, their drivers and view of the world and their business initiatives is a cornerstone and the starting point of account planning, especially in the discovery phase. 
But just knowing the initiatives is not enough. It is important to understand their strategic value (and urgency) for the customer - which is best done if you know how they relate to the customer's topline (strategic improvements, mission achievements or revenue growth) or to their bottomline (cost optimization or risk avoidance). 
The strategic value of the initiatives are one dimension that help you to prioritize the focus when account planning. The other dimension is Red Hat's ability to provide value to the customer when executing these initiatives. 
The prioritization model can be represented by a 2x2 matrix with the strategic value of Red Hat in x-axis and the impact of the initiative on customer's business in the y-axis. When identifying where to focus, try:
Defining each customer initiative known to using the 2 dimensions (value for them & RH ability to add value to the initiative)
Plotting the initiatives into the matrix and using that for prioritization:
Initiatives in the bottom left quadrant are not relevant for the customer and RH does not add value → deprioritize
Initiatives in the top left quadrant are important for the customers, but RH does not have much potential to contribute → we should be aware of these initiatives (as they will probably tie in customer's budget, resources and focus), but should not focus on them since our value will not be sufficient (see if this is opportunity to have the partners leading?)
The top right quadrant is the sweet spot where RH value is high and the value of the initiative for the customer is also high → any initiative in this space is probably containing many potential opportunities
The bottom right quadrant is tricky: RH can add great value to initiatives in this space, but their value for the customer is not high, which can cause that they might be deprioritized; work in this space only in case that these initiatives have budget allocated for the current year and a clear, strong sponsor
After you have identified initiatives that should be our priority focus with the customer, run a deep dive discovery with your extended team, with the partners and with your contacts inside of the customer. Break the initiatives into smaller pieces - projects that can support the main objectives of the initiatives and select those that RH can add value to. This is the crucial moment when the account planning converts into discovery of tangible business opportunities!

       


1. Start with customer

Understand their key initiatives, their success metrics and targets



       


2. Find how we fit

Identify where we fit with the value we can deliver to the customer - this is critical for prioritization



       


3. Best way to execute

Use the outcomes of the prioritization exercise to align your team and execute together.


From the high-level customer initiatives drill down to concrete projects that could power it. This is the discovery process of converting the strategic account planning into real business opportunities.

Relate it to the value of RH for the customer initiative on the x-axis

BUILD YOUR STRATEGY: Setting account ambition targets
Setting up ambitious targets for the account is the core of the planning activity. The objective is to aim high, but still keep the goal attainable. 
For the one year account goal and growth, you can use as the basis of your estimate the quota set for the account. For the 3-years goal and growth, you will need to make a prediction. 
What to use as the basis for your prediction::
Estimate of the total addressable opportunity:
Leverage your knowledge of the customer's initiatives  and any information about budget allocated to them (and our potential attainable share)
Leverage any information about the customer's IT budget (use Finlistics, marketing data or analysts estimates)
Leverage any data about business the customer is doing with our competitors and partners
Estimate of the potential growth:
Extrapolate potential IT spend growth from the customer's financial data for past couple of years
Leverage 6sense or LinkedIn Sales Navigator to understand the rate of headcount growth of the customer and growth of jobs focused on specific technologies
Understand any investments the customer has obtained, M&A, expansion decisions or similar.
Analyze the industry and market trends
Investigate any data about the trend of business the customer does with our partners
Analyze our own business trends:
Check PitchBuilder to review our renewal  base & customer's interactions with our resources
Check the historical growth and composition of our business and the available pipeline, average deal size, win rates etc. 
Understand the adoption of our technologies by the customer
Understand the intensity of customer engagement & relationship level - frequency and levels of contacts are solid precursors for future business:
Review NPS & verbatim comments as well as any other customer feedback from the Experience Hub
Check the people.ai Engagement Score for the customer
Understand the progress of our key stakeholder engagement activities & marketing actions
Cooperate with other teams in RH and with your management on this activity - there is a wealth of knowledge internally from similar industries, similar customers, successful and lost opportunities etc. that will help you to set accurate goals. 
Our goals for current year & 3 years should be backed by understanding that there are customer initiatives to which RH can add value and have potential that is 2-3x higher than our ambitions. 
The Whitespace Map will then be showing from which TDP & services we can expect the revenue to come from. 

This enables to rally the internal resources to support the achievement of the account ambitions.

The Initiatives present the customer-centric view of the business - focusing on what the customer is trying to achieve and how they measure success. Initiatives should not blend with opportunities. You can find initiatives in the account plan navigation bar. To add a new initiative select the “Add initiative” button in the upper right hand side. 
Each initiative should have defined timeline, target value (think the share of wallet RH can get from the budget the customer has allocated to this initiative), the success metrics & base and target measures. As mentioned, try to capture these as much as possible from customer's perspective. 
Once you create your initiative, you should start tagging to them potential & existing opportunities. You can also add opportunities from a different accounts (parent - child hierarchy in RHSC or even from any account). NOTE: before you can tag opportunities, add Business Units to Whitespace Map. 
In order to provide details about customer adoption journey or track the key activities, you can use the Notes on the initiative card as well as the Actions on the initiative card. 
If properly identified, the initiatives will give you a strong perspective of the account with the customer in the center. Also, combined, the target values of the initiatives & potential opportunities attached to them should give you a solid basis for assessing the long term account ambition.

INITIATIVES:  Tagging existing opportunities
Tagging opportunities to initiatives is an important part of connecting the account planning to its execution and conversion into a real business. By tagging the opportunities you will see how much of the Target Value of the initiative (representing the attainable share of the wallet) you have been able to achieve.
It is not necessary to tag all the opportunities to initiatives - opt for what makes common sense. Tag the largest opportunities and opportunities that have a specific meaning -for example any first of a kind opportunities, even if small (e.g. your first services opportunity or POC on new product). Tag enough to showcase the progress you are driving towards your account goals and ambitions. 
There is a connection between the opportunity qualification and customer initiatives that are captured in the account plan. Understanding the customer initiatives gives you idea about the “Identify pain” for the opportunities rolling into it. Understanding how the customer measures success of the initiative is the basis for the qualification of Metrics, your key contact of the initiatives might be your Economic Buyer or Champion etc. - tagging the opportunities to the initiative is an opportunity to validate that your qualification is correct. This process helps you to ensure that your pipeline is healthy as well as discover new opportunities. 
You can tag to an initiative any opportunity that exists in RHSC. By default you will be offered opportunities that are on the account that hosts the account plan. Clear and measurable (SMART) success criteria for initiatives that will collectively deliver business outcomes defined by the customer
Critical milestones, events, and measurements that document incremental successes
Work with the customer to understand their key outcomes and the aspects that make up each one:
Overall definition of “success”
Understanding of multiple aspects that must be achieved along the way
Key dates, timelines, and deliverables
Key parameters of the initiative:
Timing: The deadline for the initiative (when it should be complete)

Base Measure: The initial value of the success metric at the start of the initiative
Target Measure: The target value of the success metric at the end of the initiative 
Status:
‘Blocked’ - initiative is currently not being progressed due to a blocker or issue
‘At Risk’ - initiative is making progress, but is not on track to achieve success criteria by the deadline
‘On Track’ - initiative is currently on track to achieve success criteria on or before deadline
Progress:
‘Not started’ - work towards achieving the initiative success criteria has not started
‘In progress’ - work towards achieving the initiative success criteria has started, but is not complete
‘Complete’ - work towards achieving the initiative success criteria has finished (either successfully or unsuccessfully)

WHITESPACE MAP: General guidance (RH-centric view of the business)
The Whitespace Map provides a RH-centric view into our business with the customer. It shows our opportunities and potential opportunities plotted between RH Technology Decision Points (TDPs) and customer's Business Units.
This view is connected to the customer Initiatives through the opportunities and potential opportunities - building thus a holistic perspective of our business with the customer. 
The lines represent the RH Technology Decision Points (TDPs) - which mark our Go-to-market (GTM) strategy. There is a line “Other” to cover any products not connected to any TDP as well as the line “Transformational Services” for the situations when we are leading business with Services more than with our product lines. 
The columns are consisting of the customer Business Units or Groups of Business Units. 
You can use Base View or a more expanded Detailed View of the Whitespace Map using the button in the upper right corner. 
Each of the fields in the resulting matrix represents a group of business opportunities, giving you a visual overview of where we are and what are our blind spots. The visual is more powerful if you leverage the “Status” field on the Whitespace Map card. In order to do so, click on any field in the Whitespace Map matrix. When the card opens on the right side of the screen, select the appropriate option from the Status dropdown menu:
Deployed = RH solution is prevalent in this segment of business with the customer
Competitive = RH solution is work in progress, we are competing to become an established solution in this segment
Competitor = RH solution is blocked by a competitor or by other obstacles that are preventing us to compete.
Open = we are exploring if our solution can be relevant in this segment
Each Whitespace Map card enables you to see the aligned opportunities and potentials, but also map Competitors (including their share of the wallet) and eventually end of their license/subscription. This function is useful for situation such as VMWare takeout (capturing expiration of the license) or for tracking hyperscaler commitments. 
In the “Available Market” section you can track the Market Value (essentially the “wallet” (=budget the customer's business unit has for this solution) and our share.

WHITESPACE MAP: Business Units & Groups (RH-centric view of the business)
In order to make the Whitespace Map and opportunity tagging work, you need to add the customer's Business Units into the Whitespace Map. 
Customer's Business Units can be defined as customer's departments, divisions, subsidiaries or similar with whom you are discussing business. They do not need to necessarily correspond to the organizational division of the customer - they should be more reflective of which units of the customer have needs/pains that they are trying to solve leveraging software. 
When plotting the business units, do not limit yourselves to just the units you normally work with. The point of the Whitespace Map is to envision where else you can go within the customer's organization to develop new business. So if you are working currently only with the IT Operations, plot at least 2 other business units that you know have a IT needs and start to develop the contacts to explore what opportunities we could have there.
If your customer is large, you can use the Business Units “Groups”. The Groups enable you to organize the business units. Most common types of groups used are:
Geographical groups (for international or global accounts)
Tier 1, tier 2 … groups (for accounts that have some high revenue departments or subsidiaries and some less spending departments/subsidiaries)
Verticals (for accounts that are strongly verticalized)
You can rearrange the business units under groups or between themselves by clicking on the “Manage Units” button in the top right corner and then dragging and dropping the tiles with the units' names. You can erase the Business Units or Groups by clicking on the trash bin icon at the right side of the tile with the business unit's name. 
Best practice when creating a Business Unit is to provide a description of the business unit. 
Each Business Unit can have attached a standalone Account Map, which is a useful function for account with a complicated structure and many contacts. 
REMEMBER:

Once you create and name a business unit, you can erase it, but cannot rename it. So think about naming convention before you create the business units and their groups.

Key Characteristics of an Economic Buyer

Identifying the True Economic Buyer (EB)
To ensure you’ve identified the true Economic Buyer—and not just a mid-level manager or budget holder—use the following criteria:
The Power of VetoThe Economic Buyer has the authority to override consensus. They can say “yes” when others say no—and just as easily say “no” when others are aligned.
Profit & Loss (P&L) OwnershipThey are directly accountable for the financial performance of their business unit or the company. Because they carry the impact of financial outcomes, they have final authority over strategic investments.
Access to Discretionary FundsUnlike standard budget owners operating within fixed limits, the Economic Buyer can reallocate or unlock discretionary funds to support high-priority, unplanned initiatives.
Strategic (Not Technical) FocusEconomic Buyers evaluate solutions through a business lens—not a technical one. Their decisions are grounded in three core questions:
What is the cost?
How quickly will we realize value (Time to Value)?
How does this reduce risk or impact the bottom line?
The Ultimate Champion Test
A deal cannot be confidently forecasted without direct engagement with the Economic Buyer.
If your internal advocate (“Champion”) is unwilling or unable to facilitate access to the Economic Buyer, consider it a significant red flag. This often indicates the individual may not be a true Champion—or lacks the influence required to drive the deal forward.
Account Planning Guidance
Economic Buyers may exist at both the opportunity level and the broader account level.
For account planning, focus on identifying account-level Economic Buyers—one or more individuals who have overarching financial authority and influence across the customer organization.

ACTIONS: Getting started with execution
The aim of account planning is to build a strategy and based on it drive action. 
The “Actions” functionality is enabling to capture actions in the flow of the account planning session. 
Actions are Salesforce tasks that are tied to the account, but also to the account plan and the objects within it - the scorecard and the initiatives.
To create an action from the scorecard, click on the grey circle with the plus sign in the upper right corner of any of the questions cards. Continuous Planning Cycle (New Standard)
Once a year the sales manager will need to approve the account plan in RHSC
It’s a best practice to be reviewing the plans with your account executives quarterly to ensure they are adding quality as well as meeting the minimum requirements
The approval cycle will be based on the customer’s fiscal year start month
~5 months before customer fiscal year start:
AE will receive automated email to start this exercise
Copy prior year account plan
Update with new requirements and priorities, mark as primary
Review with manager to align on strategy and direction
THIS is when you can approve or reject - with a reason in RHSC and once updated you will receive an automated message to review and approve
70% of our clients have a January fiscal year start, therefore the most of your approvals will occur around August/September
The rest of the year will be rolling - cadence here.
How and where to approve or reject?   ACCOUNT RECORD
Status Definitions
⏱️ Pending ReviewDefault status when manager receives the notification to review/approve
✅ Approved:Select when the plan meets minimum requirements and demonstrates strong quality, clarity, and opportunity potential (AI tools will be available to help assess minimum requirements, but Manager judgement is expected for quality)
❌ Rejected:Select if the plan is incomplete or requires improvementInclude clear guidance in the comments to help the AE address gaps
Re-Submission Process

Account Planning: What Great Looks Like
At its core, account planning is about developing a deep understanding of your customer. The better your team understands the customer, the better they can identify where we fit—and ultimately win more business.

Larger deal sizes

Stronger early-stage pipeline

Win rates improving
Average deal size increasing
Deal velocity accelerating
Early pipeline expanding
Customer consumption meeting or exceeding targets

FIELD MANAGERS:  How to read & review account plans/and resources
The key question to ask when reading & reviewing any account plan is: Does it align the customer's business objectives with our business value, and, based on this alignment, establish ambitious goals supported by a clear, actionable plan to achieve them?
In order to answer this question, a good flow of reading the account plan is the following:
Review the Customer's Initiatives, their success metrics and potential opportunities, opportunities and customer success actions tied to them:
Is it clear how can we contribute to the success of customer's initiatives?
What is the total size of the potential and existing opportunities tagged to them? 
Is it clear that the initiatives are urgent, have budget allocated and a sponsor?
Review the Scorecard to deeper dive into the customer intelligence to back up our understanding of the Initiatives:
Is our gathered customer intelligence consistent with the understanding of the customer initiatives?
Are there any gaps in what we know about the customer?
Are the information contained in the Technical & Customer Success sections supporting account outlook resulting from the potential and existing opportunities tagged to the initiatives?
Check the Whitespace Map to understand the structure of our business in terms of TDPs and alignment with customer's business units:
How extensively are we connected with the customer (are we limited to few business units centered around IT or more broadly)?
Do we see opportunities (existing, potential) for multiple RH TDPs and services?
Do we have many business whitespaces? Are we heavily blocked by our competitors?
Review the Account Plan Overview - the account ambitions, the actual account plan and its milestones and the identified account risks:
Based on what you have seen in the Initiatives - are the account ambitions justified by customer's real needs?
Is the plan consistent with the Whitespace Map and with the intelligence captured in the Scorecard? 
Check the Account Maps to understand the network of relationships and engagement levels we have with the customer and partners:
Is our level of engagement and network of relationships adequate to support the account ambitions?
Are actions related to key stakeholders of the account properly captured as part of the plan and in the Scorecard?
This flow of reading the plan will give you the ability to identify its strengths, but also any possible inconsistencies - and provide good feedback to the account team

GLOBAL ACCOUNTS AND INTERNATIONAL ACCOUNTS
You can use the functionality for account planning in RHSC to plan for a group of accounts. This is useful for global accounts or for any accounts that are connected as should be planned for as one customer (we call them International Accounts in the Global Account Planning Procedure). 
Planning for the International / Global Accounts in one plan has benefits in terms of greater alignment of the account team, ability to spot new opportunities and ability to understand the customer holistically and serve them better.
In order to be effective in planning for International Accounts, it is necessary to:
Place the account plan at the top account in the RHSC hierarchy - because this will enable you to pull into the Whitespace Map and Initiatives opportunities from all the accounts in the hierarchy (Opportunities in Child Accounts) as well as Contacts to the Account Map (Contacts in Included Accounts)
Get Read and  Write access to the account with the overarching plan to all the members of the Global/International Account Team (e.g. an NA International Account may have accounts in all geos - and APAC and EMEA account teams will need to be added to the Account Team in order to be able to contribute to the plan)
Best practice of how to use the account planning functionality for the International Accounts (pioneered by the Telco team) is:
Use the Initiatives section for all global and local customer initiatives - using naming convention for differentiation (e.g. Global Infrastructure Modernization vs. UK - Automate Everything)
Use the Whitespace Map for all the accounts:
Define the Business Units and Groups of Business Units for Whitespace Map - the tool is flexible and enables you to combine global and geographical perspective: you can for example have Group called ING Global with units called Core Banking, AI Center of Excellence etc. and then ING Australia with units called IT Operations, DevOps and Retail - the decision is more about what makes sense from the perspective of how you do business with the customer
Each business unit can have own Relationship Map with own orgchart
When created, Business Unit cannot be renamed, so be careful when deciding on the naming convention
Account Maps - you can have multiple account maps (e.g. for each customer business unit or for each initiative), but only one of them can be marked as primary and that is the one that is exported when you print out the account plan using the export function. Recommended is to use as  the primary map the one that has the top leadership (any global verticals at least with their leaders and our key champion) and the leadership of the most important subsidiaries. 
Scorecards - it is advised to have the global plan captured in the global top hierarchy account and any regional plans to have captured in their own scorecard. For easy access, provide  links to those scorecards in the Files section of the top account plan in the hierarchy.

MAKE IT HAPPEN: Red Carpet & account re-assignments
As part of the annual segmentation process, personnel changes etc.  it can happen that accounts are assigned from one account executive to another. This process is in Red Hat called Red Carpet.
This is a description of who should be doing what in the Red Carpet process and how is this work tracked in the systems:
Outgoing Account Executive
When you are informed that one of your accounts will be handed over to another Account Executive, update it (contacts, opportunities incl. MEDDPICC and Next Steps, account plan)
Once the account is transferred in Red Hat Sales Cloud, you will be contacted by the new (incoming) Account Executive to review the account and plan communication of the change with the customer
Support the new (incoming) Account Executive in the communication with the customer to ensure no disruptions to customer experience.
Incoming Account Executive:
When you are informed that you will be receiving a new account, wait until it appears in RHSC
Once it appears, go to the Accounts list view (accessible from the upper navigation bar) and find the account.
Select the account (resp. All the newly received accounts and in the upper right corner click on the button “Create Account Action Plans”. 
Once the window opens, find the Red Carpet Action Plan Template by starting to type it (called “Red Carpet Handover NEW 2024) - select it and click save.
The Red Carpet tasks will appear in the activity timeline on the account object. The tasks are sequential  - once you complete the first one, next appear. There are 3 tasks in total.
The tasks require meeting with the Outgoing AE and reviewing the account, account plan, contacts and opportunities →arranging meeting/call with the customer and relevant partners and → confirming that the handover is complete.
You can easily see all your Red Carpet tasks in the My Open Tasks list (select Tasks in the upper navigation). Use Split View for ease of update of the tasks. 
REMEMBER:
This is a collaborative process with the customer experience being its front and center. If you encounter any problems, do not hesitate and talk to your manager.

GETTING STARTED: Which accounts require Account Plan
The accounts in RHSC are clearly marked as “Requires Account Plan” leveraging a special field in the Details of the Account. 
This fields can have different values, but for the purposes of the account planning the 2 relevant values are:
Yes - the account requires an account plan
None (or empty) - the account does not require and account plan (this does not mean that there cannot be plan - we welcome voluntary planning, but the account plan will not be enforced or monitored as part of the global reporting)
This field will be pre-populated centrally (no action required from Account Executives) and used for reporting on account planning process in RHSC
What are the rules applied to identify accounts that require an account plan:
For public sector accounts, the list of accounts was pulled together by the sales leadership
For non-public sector accounts: there must be at least 1 account plan for each Global Sales Group in Enterprise, Telco and in Corporate Commercial (for the latter only for GSG with ARR over 100k USD)
Within the particular GSG:
The account plan should be located in the account that is at the top of the account hierarchy
If there is no top account or it does not make sense, the account plan is typically located in the account with highest number of opportunities
❗❗ If your account is not marked as requiring an account plan, but you think it should be (or vice versa), please contact jchvalko@redhat.com ❗❗

GETTING STARTED:  How to fill in Ambition Plan Light (Corporate only)

The Ambition Plan Light works essentially the same, including the same Minimum Documentation Requirements (you still need 2 initiatives, 1 potential, 1 business unit and 1 primary account map with 2+ contacts) , but:
